**Short narrative (for the human reviewer):**

The retrospective is unusually clean about one thing and inconvenient about everything else.

- **The one thing that works:** relative volume ≥ 2× at confirmation. Across all three thresholds it is the only factor whose lift CI clears 1 with adequate support. If you change one weight, raise 1b RVOL.
- **The trend block is mostly survivorship.** ma_stack and "30% off the 52w low" look mildly positive at lift ~1.2–1.3, but the S&P 1500 universe is current members only — names that surged from oblivion and never joined the index are simply not in the sample. Don't reward these heavily.
- **The MACD block is backwards.** Both MACD flags are contrarian and get more so at higher thresholds. The screener is rewarding a state that mostly arrives AFTER the surge has started. Reframe 1d to detect the inflection, not the completed cross.
- **The biggest finding is the coverage gap.** `within_25pct_of_high`, `price_above_ma50`, `macd_positive`, `bb_squeeze`, `low_rv_base`, and `market_regime_ok` are ALL contrarian — and they all sharpen as the surge threshold rises from +30% to +50%. The interpretation is consistent and important: the largest surges launched from names that were far off highs, below MA50, with still-negative momentum, in a NOT-risk-on macro regime. The current rubric (especially the hard MA200 and market-regime gates) structurally rejects them. Consider adding a parallel "reversal / momentum-turn" archetype rather than tweaking weights inside the existing continuation-only frame.
- **Scope caveat I want to repeat plainly:** this entire report only speaks to Dim1 and Dim5. If the contrarian pattern is real, the actual edge lives in Dim2 (catalysts) — earnings beats, guide-ups, FDA/contract news, short squeezes — which is exactly what Phase 1.5 EDGAR backfill and Phase 2 forward snapshots are designed to capture. Don't over-rotate the technical weights before that data is online.
